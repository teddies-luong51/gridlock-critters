# Game Concepts — Hybrid-Casual Shortlist

*Derived from `market-research.md` (2026-06-09). Five concepts spanning the proven-growth sub-genres, each with a deliberate differentiator. No genre was pre-specified; the data points hard at **hybrid-casual puzzle/arcade with dual monetization and a meta layer**.*

---

## Executive Summary — Top Pick

**Recommended lead concept: Concept 1 — "Critter Cargo" (spatial-puzzle core + collection/cozy meta).**

Why: It sits in the **fastest-growing, lowest-saturation winning lane** — spatial/block puzzle (Color Block Jam is doing $42M/quarter from a standing start) — but fixes that category's biggest weakness (**meta-thin → D30 decay**) by bolting on a **cozy collection + light build meta** borrowed from Capybara Go's proven charm strategy and Royal Match's decoration-goal pull. The core is **5-second ad-legible** (the single biggest UA driver in the category), monetizes via the proven **fail-offer** model, and the cozy-animal theme has live cultural momentum. It threads the gap between "meta-thin hit that burns out" and "meta-deep hit that's expensive and pay-aggressive."

Concept 2 is the highest-ceiling/highest-risk swing; Concepts 3–5 are credible alternates.

## Concept 1: Critter Cargo

**Genre:** Spatial/block puzzle + cozy collection & build meta
**One-line pitch:** Slide animal crates out matching-color gates to clear the dock — then build a cozy sanctuary with the critters you rescue.

**Core mechanic:** A tight grid packed with colored animal crates. Drag each crate so it exits through its same-color gate; crates block each other, so order and pathing are the puzzle. Board clears in 20–40 seconds. Instant restart on fail.
**Meta hook:** Every cleared level rescues a critter into your **sanctuary** — a light decoration/build space (Royal Match's "aspiration goal" trick) plus a **collection** of animals with cosmetic variants. Daily events, streaks, a light season pass.
**Target audience:** 25–45, majority female-skewing casual-puzzle players (the Royal Match/Playrix demo), cozy-game and collection lovers; secondary broad mass-market puzzle.
**Visual direction:** Bright, high-contrast flat shapes on the board (ad-legible) transitioning to warm, cozy hand-painted sanctuary art.

**Market fit:**
- Similar to: Color Block Jam (core) + Royal Match (meta-pull) but differentiated by **a cozy collection meta on a spatial-puzzle core** — nobody is doing the spatial mash-up *with* a real retention meta.
- Gap it fills: Color Block Jam's D30 decay risk; the meta-thin spatial-puzzle hits have no collection/aspiration loop.

**Monetization angle:** Dual (IAA + IAP). **Fail offers** ("keep the crate moving" packs), boosters, sanctuary décor IAP, starter pack $0.99–$1.99, season pass. Premium-leaning ad load.

**Risk flags:**
- Spatial-puzzle core is getting crowded fast — needs a crisp visual identity to win UA creative.
- Bolting meta onto a fast puzzle adds build/tuning scope vs. a meta-thin clone.

**Confidence:** **High**
**Rationale:** Plants in the proven highest-growth lane, fixes that lane's known weakness, ad-legible core + culturally hot cozy theme = strong UA + retention combination.

## Concept 2: Capy Dungeon Run

**Genre:** Idle-RPG / roguelite mash-up (HABBY template) with visual (low-text) combat
**One-line pitch:** A cozy animal hero auto-battles through a roguelite dungeon — pick skills, collect companions, push deeper each run.

**Core mechanic:** Tap-light auto-combat; every few rooms a **roguelite choice** (skill / gear / companion) branches the run. Visual, animated encounters rather than Capybara Go's heavy text. Runs last 3–6 min.
**Meta hook:** Permanent upgrades between runs, **companion collection** (gacha-light), build experimentation, guild/social layer, weekly live-ops events.
**Target audience:** 18–40, mid-core-leaning casual players who want *agency* in idle games; strong in KR/TW (idle-RPG over-index) plus Western cozy-RPG fans.
**Visual direction:** Adorable animal mascots in a charming, readable 2.5D dungeon — cute surface, deep build guts.

**Market fit:**
- Similar to: Capybara Go (template) but differentiated by **visual (not text-based) combat + a stronger long-tail live-ops/social layer** to counter the genre's steep retention decay.
- Gap it fills: Capybara Go's text-heavy core limits Western mass reach and its retention burns fast; this targets both.

**Monetization angle:** IAP-heavy (companion/gear pulls, packs, battle pass) + rewarded ads. Highest LTV ceiling of the five.

**Risk flags:**
- Highest production scope and content-treadmill burden (live-ops or it decays like the original).
- Gacha/pay-pressure perception; needs a less hostile economy than incumbents to differentiate.

**Confidence:** **Medium**
**Rationale:** Highest revenue ceiling and rides a proven template + hot mascot theme, but it's the most expensive/operationally demanding and the genre has a known decay problem.

## Concept 3: Unscrew It! Workshop

**Genre:** Screw/sort "tidy" puzzle + light meta progression
**One-line pitch:** Unscrew and sort bolts to take apart 3D contraptions — satisfying, tactile, and endlessly tidy.

**Core mechanic:** Remove color-coded screws into matching slots to disassemble a 3D object; run out of open slots and you fail. Tactile, ASMR-satisfying, 30–60 sec levels.
**Meta hook:** Workshop progression — unlock new tools/themes, a collection of disassembled "builds," daily challenges. Deliberately moderate meta.
**Target audience:** Broad mass-market casual, 20–45, satisfaction/ASMR-puzzle seekers; very wide ad reach.
**Visual direction:** Clean, glossy 3D with chunky satisfying screws and crisp pop/click feedback.

**Market fit:**
- Similar to: Screwdom (proven $27.1M Q2 2025) but differentiated by **theme + a tidier meta and stronger juice/feedback**.
- Gap it fills: Most screw-puzzle entrants are meta-thin clones; modest meta + best-in-class feedback can separate.

**Monetization angle:** Dual model with proven **fail offers** ("add a slot" = the genre's signature converter, ~14–17% of IAP), boosters, ad-removal $2.99.

**Risk flags:**
- Most crowded/clone-heavy of the five — differentiation rests on polish and theme, which is thin defensibility.
- Screw-puzzle may be near its trend peak; entering late.

**Confidence:** **Medium**
**Rationale:** Proven monetization and broad appeal, but the clone density and late-cycle timing cap the upside.

## Concept 4: Hold the Hollow

**Genre:** Tower-defense × roguelite hybrid (emerging mash-up) — casual-ified
**One-line pitch:** Place quick traps to hold off waves, then draft roguelite upgrades to survive deeper into the night.

**Core mechanic:** Drag-and-drop traps/turrets onto lanes; auto-resolve a wave; between waves **draft one of three roguelite upgrades**. Casual input, tactical decisions. ~3–5 min runs.
**Meta hook:** Persistent hero/base upgrades, trap collection, biome unlocks, weekly events. Run-based + permanent layers.
**Target audience:** 18–40, casual-strategy and roguelite fans wanting bite-sized tactics; broad mass-market with a strategy lean.
**Visual direction:** Charming stylized "clear the darkness" fantasy — readable lanes, satisfying wave-clear juice.

**Market fit:**
- Similar to: 2025 emergents (Auto Defense, Everwarder, Lone Tower) but differentiated by **fully casual-ified, ad-legible input and a cozier theme** for mass-market hybrid reach.
- Gap it fills: TD×roguelite is *emerging* but mostly mid-core; a true hybrid-casual entry with 5-sec ad legibility is open.

**Monetization angle:** Dual model — fail/continue offers, upgrade packs, hero IAP, rewarded "draft a 4th upgrade." Battle pass.

**Risk flags:**
- TD core is harder to make 5-sec ad-legible — UA creative risk.
- Emerging sub-genre = unproven mass-market ceiling (could stay mid-core niche).

**Confidence:** **Medium**
**Rationale:** First-mover potential in a rising mash-up, but ad-legibility and unproven casual ceiling are real risks.

## Concept 5: Gridlock Critters (alternate spatial twist)

**Genre:** Spatial/jam puzzle with a fresh **gravity/rotation** twist + collection meta
**One-line pitch:** Tilt the board to slide critters home — every level flips gravity and your plan with it.

**Core mechanic:** Slide-and-jam like Color Block Jam, but you **rotate/tilt the whole board** so all pieces shift under gravity at once; route every critter to its gate before they collide. Novel-but-legible spatial hook. 30–45 sec levels.
**Meta hook:** Critter collection + light habitat build, streaks, events — same retention spine as Concept 1.
**Target audience:** Broad casual-puzzle, 20–45, spatial/logic-puzzle lovers.
**Visual direction:** Bright flat board pieces with a playful tilt animation; cozy habitat meta art.

**Market fit:**
- Similar to: Color Block Jam but differentiated by the **gravity/tilt mechanic** (a genuinely fresh spatial verb the ad creative can show in 5 sec).
- Gap it fills: A new spatial *verb* in the hottest puzzle lane, vs. the wave of color-gate clones.

**Monetization angle:** Dual model, fail offers, boosters, décor IAP, season pass.

**Risk flags:**
- Tilt-gravity may be harder to teach than direct drag — tutorial investment.
- Overlaps heavily with Concept 1's meta; pick one spatial-puzzle lead, not both.

**Confidence:** **Medium**
**Rationale:** Strong if the gravity twist proves both fresh and instantly legible in testing; otherwise Concept 1 is the safer spatial bet.

---

### Recommendation to game-director
Lead with **Concept 1 (Critter Cargo)** for the MVP — best risk/reward: proven growth lane, fixes the lane's known weakness, ad-legible, hot theme. Hold **Concept 2 (Capy Dungeon Run)** as the high-ceiling follow-up if appetite for live-ops scope exists. Treat **Concept 5** as a possible mechanic-pivot of Concept 1 rather than a parallel build.
